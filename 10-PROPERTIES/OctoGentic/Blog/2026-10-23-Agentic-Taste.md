# Agentic Taste: Why Correct Output Is Not Enough

> Part of → [[10-PROPERTIES/OctoGentic/Blog-Index|Blog Index]] ← [[10-PROPERTIES/OctoGentic/Overview|OctoGentic]]
> Published: 2026-10-23
> Word count: ~1,030

## Thesis

Autonomous systems are engineered for correctness. Tests verify it. Critics score it. But output that reaches humans is judged, not checked. Taste is the capability that bridges the gap between valid output and valuable output. It resists checklists, compounds without a ceiling, and develops through contrast, negative constraints, and comparative judgment rather than scoring.

## Key Takeaways

- **T-TT1: Distinguish correctness from taste in your validation architecture.** Correctness gates should be automatic and binary. Taste gates should be comparative, constraint-based, and regularly updated.
- **T-TT2: Feed the system contrast, not just examples of good work.** Show weak output alongside strong output. The contrast is the training signal.
- **T-TT3: Encode negative constraints explicitly.** Define what the output must avoid, not just what it must achieve. Negative constraints outlive positive specs.
- **T-TT4: Use comparative judgment wherever possible.** "Which is better?" is more reliable than "How good is this?" Build evaluation around relative judgment, as Darcron does with its blind critic.
- **T-TT5: Let taste compound through exposure, not scoring.** Do not reduce taste to a single metric. Grow it through accumulated examples, refined constraints, and repeated comparative judgment.
