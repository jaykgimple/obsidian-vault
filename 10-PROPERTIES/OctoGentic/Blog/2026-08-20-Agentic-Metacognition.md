## Core Thesis

The smartest agent isn't the one that always knows the answer — it's the one that knows when it doesn't. Agentic metacognition is the discipline of building self-awareness into autonomous systems: confidence calibration, knowledge gap recognition, and reasoning process monitoring.

## Three Layers

1. **Confidence Calibration** — Empirical grounding, calibration training, domain-specific thresholds
2. **Knowledge Gap Recognition** — Unknown unknown detection, epistemic boundary mapping, query complexity estimation
3. **Reasoning Process Monitoring** — Chain-of-thought verification, assumption surfacing, alternative consideration

## Key Takeaways

- **T-MC1: Calibrate Confidence Empirically** — Confidence scores must be validated against outcomes. An agent that claims 80% confidence should be correct 80% of the time.
- **T-MC2: Map Knowledge Boundaries Explicitly** — Agents need explicit models of what they know and what they don't. Boundary crossings trigger escalation.
- **T-MC3: Surface Reasoning Chains and Assumptions** — Every decision should expose its reasoning path, assumptions, and alternatives.
- **T-MC4: Set Domain-Specific Confidence Thresholds** — Calibrate autonomy thresholds to the cost of errors in each domain.
- **T-MC5: Monitor Calibration Drift Over Time** — Track calibration accuracy as a system health metric.
