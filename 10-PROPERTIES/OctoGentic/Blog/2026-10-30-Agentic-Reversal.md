# Agentic Reversal: How Autonomous Systems Change Their Mind Without Losing Credibility

> Part of → [[10-PROPERTIES/OctoGentic/Blog-Index|Blog Index]] ← [[10-PROPERTIES/OctoGentic/Overview|OctoGentic]]
> Published: 2026-10-30

## Thesis

Reversal is harder than prevention. Once an autonomous system commits to a course of action, unwinding that commitment requires detecting that the decision has gone wrong, measuring the cost of turning back against the cost of continuing, and communicating the reversal in a way that compounds rather than erodes trust.

## Key Takeaways

- **T-AR1: Register revisit triggers at decision time.** Every decision that matters needs a specific re-evaluation condition: after N observations, time T, or signal threshold X. Without a structural trigger, decisions freeze and stale positions compound.
- **T-AR2: Ignore sunk costs in the reversal model.** The reversal decision compares only future costs of continuing against immediate costs of reversing. Historical investment is gone and must not factor into the calculation.
- **T-AR3: Use the same threshold to exit as you used to enter.** The criteria that justified a decision are the criteria that should reverse it. Moving the goalposts at exit is how systems hide failure.
- **T-AR4: Communicate reversals with evidence, not apology.** Explain what signal changed, what threshold was crossed, and what the new direction is. A system that reverses transparently builds more trust than one that never reverses because it never admits error.
